Freedom of Open Information

Everyone deserves the right to easily access, review, and understand the software, hardware, and digital systems they own or use without unnecessary obfuscation or intentional barriers to knowledge.

← Back to Home

Why Freedom of Open Information Matters

The freedom of open information is fundamental to consumer rights, technological literacy, and innovation. When companies deliberately hide technical documentation, source code, and system specifications through proprietary restrictions, legal barriers, and misinformation, it leads to:

  • Technological lock-in: Users become trapped with proprietary systems they cannot understand or modify
  • Security vulnerabilities: Closed systems hide security flaws that bad actors can exploit
  • Stifled innovation: Developers and makers cannot improve or build upon existing technologies
  • Reduced consumer choice: Users cannot make informed decisions about their technology
  • Planned obsolescence: Intentional lack of documentation accelerates unnecessary product replacement

Our Fight for Open Information Freedom

We advocate for:

  • Mandatory publication of technical documentation and specifications
  • Right to inspect source code and understand how systems work
  • Support for open-source software and hardware initiatives
  • Protection for researchers and security auditors from legal persecution
  • Transparency in algorithmic decision-making and system behavior
  • Accessible repair and modification documentation for all products

What You Can Do

Join the fight for open information:

  • Support and contribute to open-source projects
  • Choose devices and software that prioritize transparency
  • Advocate for right-to-repair legislation that includes documentation access
  • Learn about and use open-source alternatives to proprietary software
  • Report security vulnerabilities responsibly and demand transparency
  • Share knowledge and create documentation for technology you use
  • Support makers and companies committed to openness

Who We're Fighting Against

Companies hiding technical information and blocking transparency:

  • Apple - Closed ecosystem, proprietary code, restricted access to internals
  • Google - Proprietary algorithms and hidden data practices
  • Samsung - Obfuscated firmware, locked bootloaders and source code
  • NVIDIA - Proprietary drivers, closed GPU architecture documentation
  • LG - Proprietary components and refused to share technical specs
  • Cisco - Closed-source networking equipment, licensing restrictions
  • Bose - Proprietary software locks and obfuscated repair information

Who We LOVE

Companies championing open information and transparency:

  • Dell - Provides documentation and supports open-source software
  • Lenovo - Commits to transparency and publishes technical specifications
  • AMD - Open-source driver support and published GPU documentation
  • Intel - Supports open standards and releases technical documentation
  • IBM - Pioneer of open-source, commits code to community projects
  • Raspberry Pi - Fully open, accessible, and hackable computing platform
